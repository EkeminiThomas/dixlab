# The one-user session

Before beta, take one real person from a blank screen to a published case study, and watch where they stall. One honest session teaches more than a feature list.

## Who to pick

Someone who should have a case study but doesn't: a designer, engineer, marketer or student with a project they're proud of and no write-up. Not a friend who'll be polite, and not someone who has already seen Dixlab.

## Before (5 minutes)

- Run the app (see the README), with a fresh browser profile so they start signed out.
- Have them bring one project in mind. Nothing else: no notes, no portfolio.
- Ask permission to take notes. Don't record their screen or answers unless they agree, and delete anything you keep once you've written up your notes.
- Tell them: "I'm testing the product, not you. If something is confusing, that's my fault. Please think out loud."

## The task (say only this)

> "Use Dixlab to make a case study about that project, and get a link you'd be happy to send to a recruiter."

Then stop talking. Don't point, don't explain, don't rescue them for at least 30 seconds when they're stuck.

## What to watch for

Write down the time and what happened at each of these moments:

| Moment | What to note |
|---|---|
| Landing → sign up | Did they understand what Dixlab does? Did they hesitate to create an account? |
| Finding the interview | Did they find "Start the interview", or go to templates first? |
| First question | How long before they typed anything? Did they type or talk? |
| Follow-up questions | Did a follow-up help, or annoy them? Which questions did they skip? |
| "What did you cut?" | Could they answer it? This is the question that shows their thinking. |
| The draft | First reaction to the draft. Did they say "that sounds like me"? What did they rewrite first? |
| Template and customise | Did they change the template? Was that useful, or a distraction? |
| Publish | Did they find Publish? Did they understand who can see the link? |
| The link | Did they open it? Would they send it? Who to? |

Note every **stall** (more than 10 seconds without progress), every **question they ask you**, and every **workaround** (copying text elsewhere, going back, refreshing).

## After (10 minutes)

Ask, and write down their exact words:

1. "In one sentence, what is Dixlab for?"
2. "Which part felt slow or pointless?"
3. "Does the case study sound like you? Which line doesn't?"
4. "Would you send this link? To whom?"
5. "What would you have used instead of Dixlab?"

## Turning it into decisions

- List the stalls in order of how long they lasted. Fix the longest one first.
- Anything they didn't use (templates, customise) isn't a reason to add more of it.
- If the draft didn't sound like them, collect the lines they rewrote. Those show how the interview or the drafting should change.
- Repeat with a second person only after fixing the top stall.
